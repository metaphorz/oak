How the model works

    

      
Salt pruning

      
On barrier islands, onshore wind carries salt aerosol off the surf. Salt that lands on the windward shoots kills buds and young leaves, while the leeward side keeps growing. Over decades the crown becomes a smooth, hedge-like ramp that rises away from the sea, and branches curve leeward. Salt-pruned trees also stay smaller than sheltered ones.

      
The model uses an inclined shear surface whose height rises leeward at the crown shear angle. Each growing shoot that crosses it is likely to die back. Dieback triggers a dense tuft of short shoots just below the surface, which forms the clipped crown edge. It also leaves dead grey stubs and salt-burned leaves on the exposed face.

      
Wind over time

      
Every branch has a birth year and its own growth curve, so the Age slider and the time-lapse replay the tree's life. The following effects build up with age:

      
    
        
  • The trunk and limbs lean leeward.
    • 
        
  • Branches growing into the wind are stunted (flagging).
    • 
        
  • The root plate is reinforced along the wind axis: tension roots windward and compression roots leeward.
    • 
      

      
The trunk base shows that buttressing as an elliptical, fluted flare. Turn on X-ray root plate to see it below the sand.

      
Low centre of gravity

      
Live oaks branch close to the ground. Their massive, nearly horizontal limbs can sag until an elbow rests on the soil, then turn upward again. This keeps the centre of mass low against hurricane loads. You can tune it with First limb height, Limb angle and Limb sag.

      
Growing toward the light

      
A tree grows toward its whole light field, not just upward. The Light seeking slider combines three sources:

      
    
        
  • diffuse skylight from the open dome above;
    • 
        
  • the sun's path, which at Amelia Island (about 30.6°N) crosses the southern sky, so crowns grow fuller and reach further on the south side;
    • 
        
  • over open water, the extra sky from the canopy gap plus light reflected off the surface (strongest at low sun angles), which lights low limbs from the side and below.
    • 
      

      
Shoots bend toward the brighter direction (phototropism), and leaves survive longer where light is stronger. Over decades the crown flows toward the light. Limbs that reach the water aren't "drinking": they are reaching for the light, then bowing under their own weight. The roots are what actually grow toward the water.

      
Spanish moss

      
Tillandsia usneoides is an epiphytic bromeliad, not a moss and not a parasite. It hangs from near-horizontal, humid, sheltered limbs, so the model places fewer clumps on the salt-blasted windward face.

      
Renderers

      
Three.js grows and meshes the tree in real time. The same skeleton (branch polylines, radii, leaves and moss strands) is sent to Blender, which rebuilds it with 4K photoscanned bark, real hair-curve moss and Cycles path tracing. Unreal Engine 5 imports Blender's export into a Lumen-lit level with foliage wind.
